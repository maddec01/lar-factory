# Write prose

Read once before writing chat, questions, issues, briefs, PRs, commits, docs or
skills, including after handoff. Code is outside scope. For agent-document edits,
also read [Write agent docs](write-agent-docs.md). Delegated reports use the
short rules in [Orchestration](orchestration.md).

Rule numbers stay stable; removed rules leave gaps.

7. Use plain words instead of AI vocabulary such as "delve", "pivotal", "showcase" or "tapestry".
8. Say "is" or "has" instead of "serves as", "stands as" or "boasts".
9. State the point directly instead of "not just X, but Y".
10. Use the natural number of ideas, not forced groups of three.
11. Keep one term for one concept instead of cycling synonyms.
12. Use "from X to Y" only for a meaningful scale; otherwise list the topics.
13. Use periods or commas instead of em dashes, parentheses or substitute dashes.
14. Use colons before lists/examples, not as mid-sentence connectors.
15. Use bold sparingly.
16. Replace bold-label lists that repeat their label with prose. A lead-in naming an item may introduce new detail.
17. Use sentence-case headings.
18. Remove decorative emojis.
19. Use straight quotes.
20. Remove chatbot phrases and closing offers.
22. Respond directly without praise or flattery.
23. Cut filler: "to", not "in order to"; "because", not "due to the fact that".
24. Keep only the hedge the evidence needs.
25. End with specific facts or plans, not generic conclusions.
26. Replace abstract metaphor nouns with the actual mechanism: "base", "method" or "limit", not "substrate", "vector" or "ratchet".
27. Name what something does, with concrete instructions, facts or numbers. Cut claims that could describe any project unchanged.
28. Split dense sentences; keep one idea per sentence.
29. Prefer active voice and name the actor, unless unknown or irrelevant.
30. Replace weak verbs and adverbs with a stronger verb or measured number.
31. Prefer familiar words: "use", "help", "many", "if".
32. Replace aphorisms, rhetorical fragments, personified code and flourishes with literal statements.
33. Keep whole sentences, articles and verbs. Spell out arrows and abbreviations rather than making readers decode compressed prose.
